How a CBG plant works

From waste to fuel in four simple steps.

No burning and no smoke. Nature does most of the work; we make it fast, clean and safe.

  2. 01

    Collect

    Crop stubble, cattle dung, press mud and food waste come in from nearby farms, dairies, mills and markets.

  3. 02

    Digest

    Inside sealed tanks, natural microbes break the waste down without air and release biogas.

  4. 03

    Clean & compress

    We remove CO₂, moisture and impurities to get 90%+ pure methane, then compress it into CBG.

  5. 04

    Use

    CBG fuels vehicles, factories and kitchens. The leftover slurry becomes rich organic manure for farms.

Nothing goes to waste: the manure goes back to the same farms that gave us the waste.

Why it matters · Why now

India is going green — fast.

India has pledged to reach net-zero by 2070, and the country is backing clean fuels made at home. Every CBG plant, solar project and carbon credit moves that goal closer and keeps money in the country.

Burn it, or sell it? Every winter, burning crop stubble clouds the skies of North India. A CBG plant gives farmers a better choice.

It's biogas made from organic waste, cleaned to more than 90% methane and compressed. It's chemically the same as natural gas, so it works just like CNG in vehicles, factories and kitchens. The difference is that it's made from waste instead of fossil gas.

Ethanol is a fuel made from plants: sugarcane, grains or crop residue. Blended into petrol, it cuts emissions and reduces India's oil imports. India's petrol already carries 20% ethanol.

Methanol is a simple liquid fuel and chemical. Made from biomass, biogas or captured CO₂, "green" methanol can power ships and engines and replace fossil methanol in industry.

One carbon credit stands for one tonne of CO₂ (or its equivalent) kept out of the atmosphere. Projects that cut emissions can earn credits once they're verified, then sell them to organisations that want to offset their own emissions.
